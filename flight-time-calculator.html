How to work out your arrival time

Departure and arrival times on a ticket are both local, but the flight duration is real elapsed time. To find the arrival time, convert the departure to UTC, add the duration, then convert to the destination's offset.

New York to London, 7 hours. You leave at 8:00 PM on 15 January, when New York is UTC−5. That is 01:00 UTC on the 16th. Add 7 hours to get 08:00 UTC. London is on UTC+0, so you land at 8:00 AM on the 16th.

Tokyo to Los Angeles, 10 hours. You leave at 5:00 PM on 15 January, which is 08:00 UTC. Add 10 hours to get 18:00 UTC. Los Angeles is on UTC−8, so you land at 10:00 AM on the 15th, seven hours before the time you took off.

How to work out the real flight duration

Subtracting the two times on a ticket gives the wrong answer, because they are on different clocks. Convert both to UTC first.

Madrid to New York, 10:00 to 12:30 in January. It looks like 2 hours 30 minutes. Madrid is on UTC+1, so you leave at 09:00 UTC. New York is on UTC−5, so you land at 17:30 UTC. The flight takes 8 hours 30 minutes.

When the date changes the answer

The gap between two cities can change during the year. New York and London are normally 5 hours apart, but the US changes its clocks about three weeks before the UK in spring. In mid-March 2026 they are only 4 hours apart, so a time worked out from memory would be an hour off. This calculator uses the date you enter.

It does not know your flight. It has no schedules, delays or routes, so use the duration on your ticket. For a trip with connections, use the flight route map, which handles each leg and layover.

Frequently asked questions

Does flight time include the time difference?+

No. The duration on a ticket is the real time you spend travelling, and it does not change with time zones. The time difference only changes the local clock you land under.

Why does my flight land before it took off?+

Flying west across several time zones can move the local clock back by more hours than the flight lasts. A 10-hour flight from Tokyo to Los Angeles lands at an earlier clock time than it left, usually on the same date.

Are arrival times on a ticket local?+

Yes. Airlines show departure in the local time of the departure airport and arrival in the local time of the arrival airport.

Does it account for daylight saving time?+

Yes. It uses the rules for the date you enter, in both cities. If the clocks change on your travel date, check the result against your ticket.

Do I need a flight number?+

No. It works only from the times and duration you enter. It has no live flight data, so check real schedules with your airline.
